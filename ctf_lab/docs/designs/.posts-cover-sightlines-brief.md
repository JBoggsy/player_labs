## 1. The problem, in the human's words

James's framing (verbatim intent): beacon can follow battle plans in the literal sense, but is
focused on *standing at waypoints* rather than understanding area control, supporting fire, and
sight lines. He wants two concepts beacon probably already partially has — **cover** and
**sightlines** — and wants them to drive a **spreading instinct**: when beacon agents arrive near
a target point they should spread out to covered locations from which they can peek out into
useful sightlines. The waypoint should be a **suggestion**; bots should latch onto cover points
and sightlines near it — *slightly behind it if holding, ahead of it if pushing*.

His observation: around frames 350-500 the pushers move up to the top lane as the battle plan
instructs, but they arrive clumped together, neither behind cover nor looking down useful
sightlines.

## 2. Evidence: the complaint is real, and quantified

Measured from replay data **already on disk** (no new evaluation batch was needed): the v31 A/B
arms were fetched with artifacts, so the bundle `ctf_lab/scratch/eval_v31_ab/plan_win_bundle.json`
was used directly. That episode is **beacon:v31 vs ctf-focusfire:v56, coworld ctf 0.7.91**, 8v8,
beacon on the even slots (0,2,4,…,14) = seats 0-7. The "pushers" group in the active plan
`staged_push_top` is seats 3-7, i.e. slots 6,8,10,12,14.

### 2a. Spacing (the clumping)

Pusher-group nearest-neighbour spacing and forward visibility through the push:

| engine tick | mean x | min nearest-neighbour | avg nearest-neighbour | free distance toward enemy (per bot) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 656 | 406 | 13 px | 42 px | 44-108 px |
| 756 | 444 | 47 px | 60 px | 56-104 px |
| 856 | 569 | 27 px | 42 px | 32-84 px |

Context for those numbers: friendly fire is **ON** in this game and has historically been
beacon's single biggest self-inflicted cost (v2 lost ~6 deaths/game to its own bullets before
the v3 fire gate). One grenade blast radius is **52 px** (GameVersion 17 widened it 40→52). So
13-47 px spacing means the group sits inside both the friendly-fire corridor and a single
grenade's splash.

Earlier ticks are even starker — at ticks 300 and 350 all eight beacon bots are still on spawn
positions with closest pairs at **12 px**, and full alive-team pairwise spacing is 12/12/12/12/36/36…

### 2b. Sightlines (the "not looking down useful lines" half)

A 16-direction free-ray fan (4 px steps, 400 px cap) was cast from each pusher's actual position.
At the top-lane hold, **0-3 of 16 directions were open past 200 px**. Representative fans (px of
free distance per direction, index 0 = east, counter-clockwise in screen coordinates):

- tick 656, slot 10 at (380,196): `[108, 308, 144, 112, 188, 12, 12, 12, 16, 24, 148, 68, 400, 52, 52, 184]`
  — 2 of 16 open past 200 px.
- tick 656, slot 14 at (496,154): 1 of 16 open past 200 px; east-facing ray dies at 44 px.
- tick 756, slot 12 at (479,156): **0 of 16** open past 200 px.

Nobody selected their position for what it can see.

### 2c. Directional cover (the "not behind cover" half — and why it's subtler than it looks)

Here is the important finding. beacon *does* have a cover concept, and by that concept the bots
**were** in cover: `mapdata.wall_mask()`-measured distance to the nearest wall was 9-22 px for
most pushers at ticks 450-856. But their east-facing rays (toward blue, where the enemy comes
from) died in 32-108 px. **The wall was between them and the threat, not beside them.**

That is not cover — that is a bot standing in a pocket, blind to the lane, with a wall absorbing
the sightline it needed. It is the exact opposite of "behind cover with a peek angle."

Root cause in code: `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/tools/bake_map.py:251-267`, `build_cover_grid` —
"Cover cells: walkable cells orthogonally adjacent to a non-walkable cell." It is
**non-directional**. It answers "is there a wall near me" and cannot answer "is there a wall
between me and the threat, with a line I can peek out along." `nav.npz` currently holds
**1713 cover cells** out of **9268 walkable cells**.

### 2d. Why the plan path has no spread at all

v25 shipped a squad spread (`squads.spread_point`) and its A/B confirmed stacking was fixed for
the squad-order path (stacked-ticks 67→5.6 per appearance vs h006, 28.6→5.9 vs focusfire). But:

- `spread_point` is applied **only** to squad ORDER points — `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/strategy.py:154`
  (`if goal in ("H","S","P"): opos = squads.spread_point(belief.seat, opos)`).
- The **battle-plan rung** (rung 3.9, added in v30) is at `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/strategy.py:190-202`
  and passes the raw resolved POI straight through: `poi.resolve` → `Intent(point=xy)`, identical
  for every member of the group.

Five pushers, one target cell. That is the 13 px spacing. **Lesson worth stating in the doc:** a
fix that lands on one movement path does not cover a later path added at the same altitude.

Also note `spread_point`'s mechanism is a blind rank-offset along y (0/±70 px, `SQUAD_SPREAD_PX`)
that then snaps to `nearest_cover` — geometry-blind about *direction*, and it is exactly the
non-directional cover grid it snaps to.

## 3. The design

Four pieces. New concept name: a **post** — a place chosen to fight *from*, defined by position
*and* the sightline it watches.

### 3.1 One baked field: 16-direction sightlines

`bake_map.py` gains a **sightline field**: for each walkable cell and each of 16 directions, the
free-ray distance, in 4 px units, capped at 400 px, `uint8`.

- Shape 16 × 83 × 155 (`GRID_H`=83, `GRID_W`=155, `NAV_CELL`=8).
- **Measured prototype bake cost: 1.5 s**; **201 KiB raw** before npz compression. For scale,
  `nav.npz` is currently 19,626 bytes on disk.
- Fits the existing artifact pattern exactly: `walkable`, `wall`, `cover`, and the four
  `flow_{steal,home}_{red,blue}` fields already bake and ship this way
  (`ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/tools/bake_map.py:270-280`, loaded by
  `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/mapdata.py`).

**Directional cover is *derived* from this field, not baked separately.** A wall beside you is
just a short ray at ±45° off the threat axis. One field, two concepts, no redundant artifact.

### 3.2 A new module: `ctf/beacon/posts.py`

Owns the vocabulary: `sightline(cell, dir)`, `cover_toward(cell, threat_dir)`,
`choose_post(belief, center, threat_dir, …)`. `mapdata.py` gains one accessor beside
`cover_grid()`.

**Why a new module** (rejected alternatives, state them): `mapdata.py` is a thin artifact loader
with no belief and no decisions; `squads.py` is comms and roles. Post selection needs belief
(danger field, enemy tracks, chat claims) *plus* map geometry, which is neither. It is also the
unit most worth testing independently.

### 3.3 Post scoring: four terms

A candidate walkable cell within a search radius of the waypoint scores on:

| term | meaning | direction |
|---|---|---|
| **reach** | sightline length along the threat axis, normalised to 400 px | higher is better — can I see them coming? |
| **cover** | flank rays at ±45° off the threat axis being *short* | shorter is better — is there a wall to duck behind? |
| **stance** | signed progress along the threat axis relative to the waypoint | **ahead when pushing, behind when holding** |
| **danger** | the existing `Belief.danger` field sampled at the cell | penalty |

Prototype weights used to produce the numbers below: `0.55 * reach + 0.45 * cover`, where
`cover = 1 - min(min(flankL, flankR), 64)/64`. Stance and danger were **not** in the prototype —
they are part of the design, and §3.3a explains why stance must exist.

**Prototype results on the real arena** (threat axis = east; search radius 110 px; top 5 posts
with a 56 px minimum separation). Full data in the geometry JSON under `posts`:

Around the phase-2 pusher waypoint **(628,59)** from `staged_push_top`:

| rank | post | score | sightline east | nearest flank wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | (548, 20) | 0.944 | 400 px | 8 px |
| 1 | (604, 20) | 0.839 | 344 px | 12 px |
| 2 | (692, 20) | 0.718 | 256 px | 12 px |
| 3 | (548, 92) | 0.658 | 192 px | 8 px |
| 4 | (652, 92) | 0.487 | 88 px | 12 px |

Around the `flank_s` target **(654,206)**: ranks 0-4 are (740,148) 0.680 / (548,220) 0.624 /
(716,252) 0.487 / (652,220) 0.481 / (652,100) 0.476.

Around `red_rally_top` **(425,162)**: (332,156) 0.641 / (340,220) 0.586 / (524,124) 0.534 /
(524,188) 0.528 / (396,228) 0.520.

**The headline comparison for the doc:** the rank-0 post at (548,20) sees **400 px** down the
top sneak corridor with a wall **8 px** off its flank. Today's bots at the same phase stand
13 px apart with **44-108 px** of forward view. No beacon version has ever occupied that cell.

Its full fan is `[400, 28, 16, 12, 12, 12, 16, 28, 264, 108, 92, 100, 124, 24, 12, 12]` —
one long lane east, walls tight on both flanks. That shape *is* the concept, visually.

### 3.3a Why "stance" must be an explicit term

The best-scoring post for the phase-2 **push** waypoint (x=628) came out at **x=548 — 80 px
*behind* it**. Sightline-plus-cover alone favours the defensive side of a corridor, because that
is where you get a long view with walls at your back. James's "ahead if pushing, behind if
holding" intent does **not** fall out of the geometry; it needs its own signed term. This is a
concrete case where the prototype changed the design.

### 3.3b Spread is a side effect, not the mechanism

The top-5 posts came out ≥56 px apart *naturally* — good firing positions are inherently
distributed. That is the right causality (bots spread because they each want a distinct good
angle, not because a force shoves them apart). **Caveat to state plainly:** if a waypoint sits in
a genuinely open area there may be no good posts and bots will bunch anyway, so
`spread_point`'s push-apart stays as the floor.

### 3.4 Threat axis: live evidence with a static prior

Default to the static prior — direction toward the enemy pedestal, reusing
`action._threat_axis` (`ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/action.py:91-102`) — then override with live evidence
when it exists: fresh enemy tracks (`Belief.enemy_tracks`), the danger field's hot gradient
(`Belief.danger`), and heard `U`/`E` shouts. Quantised to the 16 baked directions.

**Rejected: purely static (toward the enemy half).** Simple and reproducible, but wrong wherever
a lane bends — in the top corridor the real threat comes *down the lane*, not due east, so posts
would face into walls. That is close to what the replay already shows.

**Rejected: from the plan's `facing`/`via` annotations.** Appealing because it makes the human's
tactical intent load-bearing, and the schema already has those fields (traced-only today). But it
is only as good as the annotation, and most existing orders carry no `facing` at all.

### 3.5 Claiming posts: a new `K` shout

With no comms, five bots ranking the same candidates by the same function would need pure
seat-determinism to stay disjoint. James redirected this: chat makes "occupied" actually
*knowable*.

Protocol reality (from `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/chat.py:1-30`): shouts are ≤10 printable ASCII chars,
audible to **both teams** within ~247 px through walls and fog, at ±20 px jittered coordinates,
**one shout per second per player** with one live bubble each.

- Earshot is **free** here: bots are 13-47 px apart on arrival, far inside the ~247 px radius.
- The real budget is the one-bubble-per-second slot.

**New message: `K<seat><cell>` — 6 chars** ("seat s is taking the post at cell"), using the
existing `encode_cell` base-36 scheme. Arbitration becomes **C > T > G > U > K > E > P** — intel
still wins the bubble; the claim only outranks enemy-sighting chatter and the presence ping, and
only fires while a bot is approaching or settling. Re-sent on a cadence (~48 t), claims decay
(~120 t) so a dead claimant's post frees up.

Selection: take the best post **not claimed by a lower seat and not occupied by a visible
teammate**; seat order is the tiebreak *within* the live check.

**Why this shape, and the v19 lesson it encodes:** v19's rally gate used buddy *sensing* under
fog, so the state it gated on was permanently unknown and it **deadlocked** (153 wait-ticks per
agent, wins collapsed; v20 replaced it with tick-synchronised windows). The general pattern: a
coordination check on unobservable state needs either a comms channel or a clock fallback —
v19 had neither. Posts get the comms channel, and claims still decay on a clock.

**Rejected: repurpose the `P` presence ping** to broadcast the intended post. Zero new protocol,
zero extra bubble pressure — but presence becomes a lie by up to the search radius exactly when a
squad commits, and both the leader's strength table and the respawn rejoin point read it.

**Rejected: extend `P` with a claim field.** `P<seat><cell><postcell>` = 10 chars, exactly at the
hard shout cap, leaving zero headroom for any future field.

**Rejected: purely seat-deterministic (no live check).** Perfectly predictable and trivially
testable, but the score depends on live inputs (danger field), so two bots with different beliefs
rank differently and collide with nothing to correct it.

### 3.6 Facing commitment

A settled post stores its sightline direction. `_sweep_target`
(`ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/action.py:105-121`) centres the lighthouse sweep on the **post's** direction
instead of the pedestal axis, with a narrowed arc — the bot watches the lane it was placed to
watch. Any real target still preempts via the unchanged combat overlay
(`ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/action.py:446-498`); peek/duck still overrides movement
(`ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/action.py:304-364`).

This also finally gives the plan schema's `facing` field — traced-only since v30 — something real
to drive.

James chose this over position-only. His reasoning matters and should appear: a bot in a perfect
firing position facing the wrong way is worth nothing. **Rejected: position only** (smaller diff,
one variable, but the sightline half of the concept goes unrealised and the A/B likely reads
null). **Rejected: position now, facing as a follow-up iteration.**

### 3.7 Integration: exactly where it hooks in

One insertion in `strategy.py`: when a plan objective resolves and the bot is within the arrive
radius (`PLAN_ARRIVE_PX` = 60), the target becomes its chosen post rather than the raw waypoint.
**The waypoint becomes the search centre — the "suggestion" James asked for.**

Rung 3.9 keeps its altitude, so everything above it still preempts — carrying the flag, rejoin,
intercepting a thief, escorting a carrier, grenade-clear, medkit, the convert trigger — and
everything below still governs the approach: A* with the danger cost field, peek/duck micro, the
combat overlay, windup freeze, item detours. **Goalposts, not a death march** (the v30 principle,
stated in `ctf_lab/ctf/beacon/plan.py:1-31`).

Also applied to `order_hold` / `order_push` (replacing `spread_point`'s blind y-offset with real
geometry) and the static `hold_point` fallback.

## 4. Rollout, flags, validation

- Gated behind **`BEACON_POSTS`**, with a separate **`BEACON_POST_FACING`** flag, so one build
  can A/B position and facing separately. Knobs live in `config.py` per lab convention
  (env-var knobs so each iteration is attributable).
- **Ships with activation tracing** — post cell, score, threat axis, claim source, ticks-on-post.
  This is a standing lab rule: every behaviour change ships with activation tracing, because a
  null A/B is unreadable without it. Precedent: the v7 peek-fire-duck micro measured *null*, and
  only the v8 activation tracing showed the mechanism fired (duck 14.0% of alive time, peek 3.7%)
  and therefore was not the binding constraint.
- Measurable success criteria for the A/B (state these): nearest-neighbour spacing at the
  top-lane hold rises from ~13-47 px; count of pusher-bot directions open past 200 px rises from
  0-3 of 16; ticks-on-post is non-trivial (the activation check); and the outcome metric is the
  matched A/B win/draw/loss split vs focusfire:v56 and h035:v1 via the `ctf-ab` skill.
- Rebake required: `nav.npz` gains a field, so `bake_map.py` must be re-run. The arena is
  already re-bake-on-change (a standing watch item is the `arena-large` map flip).

## 5. Risks and open questions

1. **This is a large single iteration** — bake + module + chat protocol + aim change — against a
   lab discipline of one change per iteration. Mitigation: the two flags above allow attribution
   without a second build. Open question for James: accept this, or split the build?
2. **Open-area waypoints may have no good posts** (§3.3b). `spread_point` stays as the floor.
3. **Scoring weights are unvalidated.** `0.55/0.45` came from a prototype tuned by eyeball on
   three waypoints. Stance and danger weights are entirely unset. These are knobs, and the first
   A/B may mostly teach us about the weights.
4. **The `K` shout is audible to the enemy.** Shouts carry team identity and jittered position to
   both teams, so claiming a post leaks that a bot is heading to a cell. Judgement: the position
   leak is small (±20 px, and the bot is about to be visible in that lane anyway) and the same
   tradeoff was already accepted for `O`/`P`. Worth naming, not blocking.
5. **Enemy-derived threat axis can oscillate.** If tracks flicker between two directions, a bot
   could re-pick posts every few ticks. Needs hysteresis / a commit duration; the design says
   "settled post" but the dwell rule is unspecified.
6. **Facing commitment narrows the sweep**, which trades peripheral awareness for lane focus. If
   a squad all commit to one axis, a genuine flank could go unseen longer than today. The
   existing `sector_offset_brads` per-rank sector offsets partially mitigate; interaction between
   sectors and post facing is unspecified.
7. **16 directions is 22.5° granularity.** The vision cone half-angle is 45°, so a quantised axis
   can be up to 11.25° off the true threat bearing. Probably fine; unverified.
