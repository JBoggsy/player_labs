# Brief: firefight mode — intentional combat, focus fire, and target claims

**Doc type:** Design spec (implementation brief) · **Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Status:** Approved
design, ready to implement (human settled the one open fork; see §4)

**Slug:** `beacon-firefight`
**Target lab:** `ctf_lab/`, player `beacon` (currently `beacon:v33`, competing champion)

---

## 1. The problem, in the human's words

> "Right now our agents don't seem to fight *intentionally*, just *reactively*. That is, I think
> they shoot if they see an enemy, but they don't reason about how to get kills."

That read is **correct and verified in code**. beacon's entire target selection is one line —
`ctf/beacon/action.py:87-91`:

```python
def _nearest_enemy(belief: Belief):
    if not belief.enemies or belief.self_xy is None:
        return None
    sx, sy = belief.self_xy
    return min(belief.enemies, key=lambda e: (e.pos[0] - sx) ** 2 + (e.pos[1] - sy) ** 2)
```

Nearest visible enemy, aim (with velocity lead), fire when the geometric gate clears. There is
**no firefight state, no HP awareness, no target coordination, and no notion of finishing a
wounded enemy.** Combat is a reflex attached to the movement system.

The human's requested direction: when bots detect they are under fire, enter a **firefight mode**,
coordinate with squadmates about enemy positions / incoming fire / enemy health, and **focus fire
on a single target**.

## 2. Why this is the right lever now (measured)

The previous iteration (v33 "posts", now champion) closed the *positioning* gap. Two independent
measurements say the remaining gap is the fight itself:

**vs `alphashot-ghost-red-ca3e95f:v1` (division rank 1), n=10/arm, ctf 0.7.96, 20/20 episodes:**

| metric | alphashot | beacon:v33 |
|---|---|---|
| kills / deaths | 222 / 179 | 174 / 227 |
| **forward sightline reach (our own baked 32-dir field)** | 93 px mean, 84 median | **92 px mean, 84 median** |
| positions open ≥200 px | 9% | 7% |
| **kill range** | **222 px mean, 218 median** | **187 px mean, 182 median** |
| kills inside 200 px | 44% | **59%** |
| kills beyond 400 px | 4% | **0%** |
| **clustered kills** (same side, 2 within 48 t) | **101** | 61 |

Read: **ground is even; the fight is not.** Specifically —
1. **We fight too close.** We kill at 187 px while `FIRE_MAX_RANGE_PX` is **350** — we are not
   using the range our own gate already permits, and we take *zero* kills beyond 400 px.
2. **We don't concentrate.** 61 clustered kills vs their 101.
3. **Survival caps everything.** beacon's mean lifespan vs alphashot is **407 ticks** (median 331).
   That is why posts had max dwell 71 t here vs 525 t vs focusfire. Any fight improvement must
   help *within ~5 seconds of life*, or extend that life.

The same attrition pattern beat us via `ctf-h050:v1` (0% wins before posts, 20% after). Two
different opponents now beat us by out-fighting, not out-positioning.

## 3. What the game gives us (all verified this session against source + live episodes)

**Game state: ctf 0.7.102 deployed, `GameVersion 23`, arena geometry byte-identical to beacon's
pin — NO nav rebake needed.** Live-episode `game_config` confirms `visionConeDeg: 45`,
`maxTicks: 5000`, `lives: 3`, `visionBubble: 90`.

### 3.1 Enemy HP is observable — and we currently throw it away

From the game's `src/ctf/labels.nim` (`LabelPrefixHp`):

> `hp <lit>/<total>`. A distinct object centered on its player and **fog-gated with them**:
> attach it by proximity.

So **any enemy we can see, we can read the health of.** Two subtleties that MUST be respected:

- **The numerator is LIT BAR SEGMENTS, not hit points.** `LabelHpBarSegments = 3` is fixed; the
  engine maps `hp + shieldHp` onto thirds (ceil; floor of one lit segment for anyone alive). The
  game source explicitly warns that `hitPoints` also defaulting to 3 makes these *numerically
  identical today by coincidence*, and that reading the label as hit points is live drift risk.
  **Treat it as an ordinal wound level in 1..3, never as hp arithmetic.**
- **It includes shield**, so a shielded enemy reads HIGH. The bar therefore doubles as shield
  detection, and "3/3" can mean "healthy" or "wounded but shielded".

beacon's `perception.py:218-244` (`_overhead_state`) already scans every `hp ` object, then keeps
only the one nearest **us** and discards the rest. Its own docstring says *"a wounded enemy's hp is
readable intel"*. The intel arrives every frame and is dropped.

### 3.2 Enemy identity — a stable handle for naming a target

`PlayerTrack.identity` (`types.py:57-73`) is the **nameplate seat index 0-7** (alpha..theta) from
the 0.7.69 badges, association-gated and **sticky once known**. `Enemy.identity` likewise. This is
the correct handle for "which enemy" in a claim — it is stable across frames, unlike a position.
Badges are fog-gated with their player and may be `None`; the design must degrade gracefully to a
cell-based handle when identity is unknown.

### 3.3 `under_fire` already exists — the firefight trigger is free

`belief._update_under_fire` (`belief.py:287-299`): true when a fresh **heard** impact landed within
`UNDER_FIRE_RANGE_PX`. It is hearing-based, so it fires even when the shooter is invisible. Already
consumed by the `U` shout and the plan's hold-fallback.

### 3.4 GameVersion 23 facts that change fight reasoning

- **Shields BREAK on depletion** (`absorbDamage`, sim.nim:4523-4539): a shield absorbs up to 3 hp,
  and the moment it empties the shield is *gone* — carry icon, label, and the `ShieldFireSlowdown`
  (3x slower fire) all end, and an in-flight slowed cooldown re-clamps to normal. So damage into a
  shielded enemy has a **threshold**: the first 3 hp buys no kill progress but strips their armor
  AND removes their fire penalty. Focus fire on a shielded target is a *worse* investment than on
  an unshielded one at the same bar level.
- **Kills and steals extend the clock** (`floorGameClock`, `ActionClockFloorTicks = 500`): any kill
  within 500 ticks of the limit banks overtime. Aggression near time no longer risks the clock —
  it buys time. This *raises* the value of finishing kills.

### 3.5 Chat has room — measured, not assumed

Shout constraints (sim.nim): `ShoutMaxChars = 10`, `ShoutCooldownTicks = 1s`, ~247 px radius,
audible to **BOTH teams**, ±20 px jitter, carries team + shouter identity.

Measured actual usage across the 10-episode alphashot batch (80 agent-games, mean final tick 2048):
**17.3 shouts sent per agent-game against a self-limited capacity of ~68** (`CHAT_MIN_INTERVAL_TICKS`
= 30) — **25% of budget**. Breakdown: `enemy` 33%, `post_claim` 32%, `under_fire` 31%, `grenade` 3%,
`thief` 1%.

**There is room for a target-claim message without displacing intel.** Current arbitration is
`C > T > O > G > U > K > E > P`.

### 3.6 What we do NOT have

- **No kill percept.** beacon never reads `corpse ` labels (grep: zero hits in `ctf/beacon/*.py`),
  and there is no damage/kill event on the player stream. So "is my target dead?" is **not directly
  observable** — it must be inferred (track disappearance, or the fog-independent team scoreboard).
- **The team scoreboard IS fog-independent** (`squads.enemy_lives_left`): `enemy_team_score` gives
  aggregate enemy (kills, deaths). It gives *aggregate* deaths, not *which* enemy died — usable as a
  claim-release heuristic in aggregate, not per-target.
- **No FF-suppression telemetry.** There is no counter for "held fire because a teammate blocked
  the corridor". See §7 — this is a tracing gap the implementation must close.

## 4. The design (the human settled the fork)

The human's exact framing:

> "I'll settle the fork by saying a combination of the two. I think emergent from scoring rule is
> probably the primary, but it doesn't even need to be an order to break ties. If one agent shouts
> that they're firing on an enemy and other agents hear that, they should slightly prioritize that
> enemy. In the case of scoring rule ties, they fire at the same enemy, and the first person to
> shout an enemy gets to get the claim until the enemy is dead or disappears for a while. Other
> agents shouldn't try and shout other enemies."

So: **emergent scoring is primary; a heard claim is a soft bias, not an order; claims are
first-come and exclusive.** This is the posts pattern (identical scoring + chat correction) applied
to targets. Concretely:

1. **Target score** is a pure function every bot computes identically over its visible enemies.
   Because the inputs are largely shared (positions, wound level), bots naturally converge — that
   is the "emergent" primary.
2. **A heard claim adds a bounded bonus** to that enemy's score. It must be able to break a tie and
   nudge a near-tie, but **must NOT override a clearly better target** (e.g. don't abandon a
   1-segment enemy at 150 px to help on a 3/3 enemy at 340 px).
3. **Claim ownership is exclusive and first-come**: while a live claim on some enemy exists and is
   fresh, a bot that is *not* the claimant does **not** shout a claim on a different enemy. This is
   the anti-fragmentation rule the human explicitly asked for.
4. **Claims release** when the target is believed dead or has gone missing for a while.

### 4.1 Firefight mode

A per-bot state with **hysteresis** (the posts lesson: a state that flickers is worthless).

- **Enter** when `under_fire`, OR an enemy is visible within a firefight radius.
- **Stay** for a minimum dwell after the last trigger, so a peek-ducking enemy doesn't toggle it.
- **Exit** after that decay with no trigger.
- Firefight mode is an **overlay on combat, not a movement rung.** It must NOT preempt the
  priority ladder in `strategy.py` — carrying the flag, intercepting a thief, escorting, medkit,
  convert, plan, posts all keep their current altitude and meaning. Implementer: if you believe a
  movement change is required, say so and STOP; do not silently add a rung.

### 4.2 Target scoring (replaces `_nearest_enemy`)

Score each visible enemy; pick the max. Terms (all normalized, weights as env knobs):

| term | rationale |
|---|---|
| **wound** | lower lit-segment count is better — finishing a wounded enemy converts damage into a kill, and a kill now also banks clock (§3.4) |
| **range band** | prefer the *effective* band rather than "nearest". We kill at 187 px under a 350 px gate and take 0% of kills beyond 400 px — this term exists to stop the drift into close trades. Penalize very close AND beyond-gate; peak in the mid band. |
| **claim bonus** | bounded bias toward a squadmate's claimed target (§4, rule 2) |
| **shootability** | clear LoS now (`mapdata.ray_clear`) and no teammate in the corridor (`_teammate_blocks_shot`) — an unshootable target should not win, since selecting it wastes the engagement |
| **aim cost** | brads of traverse from current aim; the gun locks aim at trigger pull and turning takes `AIM_TURN_RATE`=5 brads/tick, so a target behind us costs real ticks |
| **shield penalty** (optional, GV23) | a shielded enemy needs 3 hp of throwaway damage first (§3.4); slightly deprioritize at equal bar level |

**Do NOT reintroduce "nearest" as the dominant term** — that is the current behavior and the thing
being fixed.

### 4.3 The claim shout

A new message naming a target. Two candidate encodings — implementer picks and justifies:

- `F<seat><identity>` (4 chars) — names the enemy by nameplate index. Compact and stable, but
  useless when the badge hasn't resolved.
- `F<seat><cell>` (6 chars) — names the enemy's cell, like every other message. Always available;
  needs position→target association on receipt (fuzzy, and the enemy moves).

**Recommendation: support identity when known, fall back to cell.** If that costs a distinguishing
character, prefer two prefixes over an ambiguous parse. Keep ≤10 chars.

Arbitration: insert **below** live intel and **above** enemy chatter/presence, i.e.
`C > T > O > G > U > K > F > E > P`. Rationale: a target claim is more actionable than the generic
`E` sighting (which it partly supersedes during a firefight) but must never displace carrier/thief/
grenade/under-fire. **Do not demote any existing message** — that was a real defect caught in the
posts iteration (my spec then omitted `O` and would have silently demoted it).

Send policy: only while in firefight mode with a live target; rate-limited; and **suppressed
entirely when a fresh claim from another seat on a different enemy exists** (rule 3).

### 4.4 Claim lifecycle

- **Acquire**: no fresh claim exists for any enemy → the bot claims its own chosen target.
- **Hold**: re-broadcast on a cadence while still engaging it.
- **Release**: target believed dead, or missing for a decay window, or the claimant dies.
- **Believed dead**: no kill percept exists (§3.6). Infer from the target's track going stale past a
  threshold; optionally corroborate with the fog-independent scoreboard (enemy aggregate deaths
  increasing). Be explicit and conservative — a wrongly-held claim on a dead enemy is worse than a
  slightly late release, because it drags the whole squad's fire onto a corpse's last position.
- Claims decay on a clock regardless (the v19 lesson: any coordination gate on unobservable state
  needs a clock fallback, or it deadlocks).

### 4.5 The friendly-fire tension — name it, measure it

**This is the biggest risk in the design and it is inherent, not incidental.** Friendly fire is ON;
a shot is hitscan and stops at the FIRST body it crosses; `_teammate_blocks_shot` holds fire when a
visible teammate is within `FRIENDLY_FIRE_CORRIDOR_PX` (22) of the shot ray and closer than the
target. **Focus fire deliberately converges multiple bots' rays onto one point**, which makes
mutual corridor-blocking *more* likely — the exact failure that cost beacon:v2 ~6 deaths/game and
motivated the gate.

Mitigations available: posts already spread firing positions (v33 ships this); `separation_bias`
remains the floor; the `shootability` scoring term (§4.2) declines targets we can't currently hit.
**Requirement: the implementation MUST add a counter for fire suppressed by the FF gate** (§7) so
we can tell "focus fire didn't help" from "focus fire made us hold fire".

## 5. Explicit non-goals

- **No movement changes.** Not a new rung, not a repositioning behavior. Posts own positioning.
- **No new weapon/item logic.** Grenade and spray/arc behavior unchanged.
- **No leader/order protocol.** The human explicitly chose emergent + soft claims over orders.
- **No change to the aim/fire geometry** (`_fire_gate`, lead aim, windup freeze) beyond *which
  target* is chosen and any range-band preference expressed through scoring.
- **No rebake.** Arena geometry is unchanged at 0.7.102.

## 6. Flags and defaults

Follow the v33 precedent exactly: env knobs in `config.py`, **defaults OFF** so the image is inert
until an arm enables it, so one image serves every A/B arm via `--secret-env`.

- `BEACON_FIREFIGHT` — master gate for firefight mode + the new target scoring.
- `BEACON_FOCUS_CLAIMS` — the `F` claim shout + claim consumption, **independently gateable** so
  the A/B can separate "better target choice" from "coordination". This mirrors
  `BEACON_POSTS` / `BEACON_POST_FACING` and was what made the posts A/B readable.
- Scoring weights, radii, dwell/decay windows, and the range band all as `BEACON_FF_*` knobs.

## 7. Activation tracing (mandatory — a standing lab rule)

Every behavior change ships with activation tracing, because a null A/B is unreadable without it.
Precedent: v7 peek-fire-duck measured null; only v8's tracing showed the mechanism *was* firing
(duck 14.0% of alive time) and therefore wasn't the constraint. In the posts iteration, tracing is
what proved the stance term worked mechanically despite a null outcome.

Trace at minimum:

- firefight mode transitions + cumulative ticks in mode;
- chosen target (identity or cell), its score, and **the decomposed terms** (wound / range / claim /
  shootability / aim cost) — decomposition is what made the posts null diagnosable;
- target **switches per engagement** (thrash detector);
- claims sent / heard / suppressed-by-exclusivity, and claim release reason;
- **fire suppressed by the FF gate** (§4.5) — new counter, does not exist today;
- **kill range distribution** — this is the primary mechanism metric, since 187 px vs 222 px is the
  measured gap. Report it as a distribution, not a mean.

## 10. Known risks to state in the plan

1. **FF suppression** (§4.5) — inherent tension; must be measured.
2. **Survival ceiling** — 407-tick mean lifespan vs alphashot means a slow-converging coordination
   scheme may never pay off. Prefer fast convergence over optimality.
3. **hp label semantics** — ordinal segments including shield, not hit points (§3.1). Getting this
   wrong silently corrupts every wound-based decision.
4. **No kill percept** (§3.6) — claim release is inferential; a stuck claim drags squad fire onto a
   corpse.
5. **Claim exclusivity vs multiple simultaneous firefights** — with 8 bots spread across a map,
   two genuinely separate firefights may be happening. A global "only one claim at a time" rule
   would break the second fight. The exclusivity rule should be scoped by *earshot / locality*,
   not globally — flag this and choose deliberately.
6. **Both teams hear shouts.** A claim tells the enemy which of them we're focusing (and ±20 px
   where they are). Same tradeoff already accepted for `O`/`P`/`K`; worth naming, not blocking.
