How Gods of the Arena heroes earn, lose, and spend XP and gold

For James, before we design the farming and shopping behavior of the first James Botts policy. The research used Metta-AI/polyworld 1d7eb723. The league runs 5422fb0c (checked again today in the coworld manifest). Of the files this report cites, only the simulation module differs between the two commits, in footman-pathing and tower-collision hunks that contain none of the lines cited here. The other cited files are byte-identical. Since then origin/main moved to d915109, which lowers tower HP and damage. The live build does not include that change.

Executive Summary

Gods of the Arena has one income mechanism. A hero receives XP and gold only when its own damage is the hit that takes an enemy footman, hero, or tower to zero HP1. The rewards are fixed per victim kind: 25 XP and 15 gold for a footman, 150 and 100 for a hero, and 100 and 75 for a tower1. The fort pays nothing. Kills by towers or footmen pay nothing to anyone, and the kill of an ally pays only that ally. There are no assist rewards, no passive income, no shared team payout, and no denies. Every damage source that a hero controls counts equally. The basic attack, an explicit or automatic ability cast, and a poison potion all go through the same reward function.

Neither resource is ever lost. XP only increases, and the only gold decrease in the simulation is a shop purchase1. Death costs 216 ticks (9 simulated seconds) of absence and nothing else. Level, XP, gold, and inventory all survive respawn1. XP has exactly one use: levels. Each level increases max HP, max mana, basic-attack damage, and move speed by a fixed per-class step, up to level 202. Ability damage, ability healing, attack range, and attack speed do not change with level. Gold has exactly one use: the 20-item shop, which works from anywhere on the map. The level curve needs 14,725 XP for level 20. The default creep supply gives a team near 18,000 XP over a full match, so levels are a contest for last hits, not an entitlement. The results file reports lifetime XP as total_xp beside the binary win score, but it does not change that score3.

1 · Starting state and the two resources

  • Every hero starts at level 1 with 0 XP and 150 gold1.
  • A hero tracks three counters: xp (progress toward the next level), totalXp (lifetime), and gold1.
  • A policy can read selfLevel and selfGold, but neither XP counter4.

The Hero record holds level, xp, totalXp, and gold as plain integers1. xp is the quantity collected since the last level-up. totalXp never resets, and it is the value that the platform reports at the end of the episode. Gold is one balance, with no separate "banked" quantity. The host shows selfLevel and selfGold to BASIC, but not xp or totalXp. Thus a script can only estimate its XP progress from a count of its own kills4.

2 · How XP and gold are earned

  • The only income event is a killing hit by a hero: the hit that takes an enemy footman, hero, or tower from positive HP to zero or below1.
  • Rewards are flat per victim kind: footman 25 XP / 15 gold, hero 150 / 100, tower 100 / 75. The fort pays nothing1.
  • Basic attacks, abilities (explicit or automatic), and poison potions all pay through the same function. Tower and footman kills pay nobody. There are no assists, no team share, no passive income, and no denies.

2.1 The reward function

The engine calls applyHeroHit with the attacker, a damage quantity, and the target1. For a footman, hero, or tower target, it records whether the target was alive before the hit, then subtracts the damage. If the target was alive and is now at or below zero HP, it calls gainRewards on the attacker with the constants for that victim kind, and adds the gold to the attacker's GoldMetric. For a fort target, it subtracts HP and does nothing else. The constants are defined once1:

VictimXPGoldNotes
Footman251560 HP, so a level-1 hero needs 2 or 3 hits1
Hero150100The victim is out for 216 ticks (section 5)
Tower100751,200 / 2,400 / 4,800 HP for outer / inner / gate1
Fort00400 HP. Its destruction ends the game1

gainRewards adds the XP to xp and to totalXp, adds the gold, and then applies as many level-ups as the new XP pays for1. Nothing else in the simulation calls gainRewards.

2.2 Which damage sources qualify

Three hero-controlled damage sources reach applyHeroHit:

  • Basic attacks. The combat update lands one hit per swing, at 45% of the swing duration. The damage comes from heroAttackDamage: class base damage, plus per-level growth, plus equipment bonuses1.
  • Abilities. Every strike ability resolves through hitSpellTarget, whether the script cast it with castTarget or castPoint, or the engine cast it on its own in combat. That procedure calls applyHeroHit with the flat damage of the ability1. An area spell calls it once per object inside the footprint1, so one ultimate can collect several footman bounties at the same time.
  • Poison potion. useItem on a poison potion applies 35 damage to the current attack target of the hero through applyHeroHit1 2. Thus a potion can take a last hit.

Two damage sources never pay anyone:

  • Towers subtract HP directly. When a tower kills a hero, the engine increments the team kill counters and records a death with attacker -1. It pays no XP or gold to any hero1.
  • Footmen also subtract HP directly, for footmen, heroes, towers, and the fort1. A creep wave that kills a tower earns the team nothing, except that the next structure is now exposed.

There is no deny mechanism, because an attack on your own footman is rejected as a non-enemy target1. There is no shared team gold, no periodic income, and no reward for damage short of the kill. Assists exist only as a metric (section 7).

3 · What XP does: the level curve

  • The XP needed to leave level L is 100 + (L - 1) * 75. Level 20 is the cap1.
  • Reaching level 20 from level 1 takes 14,725 XP in total.
  • Each level-up increases max HP, max mana, basic-attack damage, and move speed by fixed per-class steps. Current HP and mana increase by the same step2 1.
  • Abilities, attack range, attack speed, and cooldowns do not change with level.

xpForNextLevel(level) returns 100 + (level - 1) * 751. gainRewards loops while the hero is below HeroMaxLevel (20) and has at least that much xp. Each pass subtracts the threshold and increments the level, so one large reward can give several levels1. After level 20, XP still collects in xp and totalXp, but it has no effect.

The cumulative XP to reach level L+1 from level 1 is 100 L + 75 L (L-1) / 2. One hero kill (150 XP) pays for the first level alone. By level 10, a hero kill is less than a fifth of a level.

On each level-up, refreshHeroStats computes max HP and max mana again from class, level, and equipment, and adds the increase to the current values. Thus a level-up in the middle of a fight is a small heal1. The engine reads basic-attack damage and move speed from class and level at the time of use1. The per-class steps are in Appendix B. Ability specs hold flat damage, heal, restore, manaCost, and cooldown values, with no level term, and hitSpellTarget applies spec.damage directly2 1. Attack cadence (attackTicks) and range are class constants2.

4 · What gold does: the shop

  • Gold is spent only through buyItem, from anywhere on the map, at any level1.
  • Four consumables (heal, heal, mana, poison) and sixteen equipment pieces. Prices go from 30 to 190 gold (Appendix A).
  • Equipment gives flat max HP, max mana, basic damage, or move speed while held. Duplicates are rejected, and nothing can be sold1.

purchaseReason accepts a purchase when the hero is alive, has at least the cost of the item, and has an empty inventory slot or a non-full stack (up to 8) of the same consumable1. applyBuyItem then subtracts the cost. This is the only place where gold decreases1. It also refreshes the stats, so equipment applies at once. Equipment bonuses are the sum over the held items1. A hero cannot hold two of the same equipment, cannot sell, and has six slots. Thus the equipment budget is six pieces, or fewer when consumables occupy slots.

Consumables convert gold to HP, mana, or damage on use: a ration is 30 gold for 40 HP, an elixir 50 for 90, a mana potion 45 for 60 mana, and a poison 40 for a 35-damage strike on the current target2. A heal on a full resource is rejected. Per point, the elixir is cheaper (0.56 gold per HP against 0.75). The agent of another entrant made the same point in the forum5. The only advantage of the ration is that you can afford it earlier.

Equipment is the durable sink. The cheapest damage is Leather Gauntlets (70 gold, +4). The most damage per item is Battle Axe or Rune Crossbow (180, +14). Knight Armor is +120 max HP for 160. The starter script already buys gear for its class as soon as it can afford each tier, so gold rarely sits idle in the baseline6.

5 · How resources are lost

  • XP is never lost. Gold is lost only by spending it.
  • Death removes the hero for 24 death-animation ticks plus 192 respawn ticks. Then the hero returns to the spawn point with full HP and mana1.
  • Level, XP, gold, inventory, and script memory all survive death. On respawn, ability charges are full and cooldowns are clear.

The simulation decreases these counters in exactly two places: hero.xp -= xpForNextLevel(...) when a level is consumed1, and gold -= spec.cost on a purchase1. totalXp and level only increase.

Thus death is a cost in time, not in resources. A hero at zero HP enters Dying, which cancels its targets and movement1. After HeroDeathTicks (24) plus HeroRespawnTicks (192), the hero respawns at its barracks with full HP and mana, no target, clear cooldowns, and every ability slot at full charges1. If the killer is a hero, it gains 150 XP and 100 gold. The victim loses nine simulated seconds of farming and map presence, and gives the lane to the enemy team. A hero kill is worth six footman kills, and a footman wave arrives every ten seconds. As a result, a death costs about one wave of income plus the bounty it gave away.

6 · Supply: how much is on the map

  • Footmen spawn 2 per lane per team every 240 ticks by default, 12 per wave, with a cap of 120 living footmen1 7 1.
  • A team faces 6 enemy footmen per wave. That is 150 XP and 90 gold per wave if it takes every last hit.
  • A default 28,800-tick match has 120 waves. That is about 18,000 XP and 10,800 gold per team from creeps, plus 900 XP and 675 gold from the nine enemy towers.

spawnWave operates every spawnIntervalTicks (default 240, ten seconds). It places two footmen at each of the three barracks pairs for both teams, unless the living count will go above UnitCap1. Each footman has 60 HP and does 12 damage per swing1.

Two things follow. First, the creep economy is shared by five heroes and it is finite. If the team divides the footman kills equally, each hero gets about 3,600 XP over a whole match, exactly the cost of level 10, and about 2,160 gold. Level 20 for one hero (14,725 XP) needs most of the creep income of the team, or a steady flow of hero kills. Second, hero kills are the only income that grows with aggression. A kill every 216 ticks against the same victim earns three times more than the creeps of one lane. The timeout rule in the guide, a game with no fort kill scores zero for all ten seats8, means that gold and XP totals are a means, never the objective.

Towers also decide who gets the income. A tower attacks the nearest enemy footman it can see first, and only targets heroes when no footman is in range. It hits once per second for 28 / 56 / 112 by tier, at 5 / 5.5 / 6 tiles1. Tower kills pay nobody, so a footman that dies under a tower is income lost to both teams.

7 · Bookkeeping: metrics and end-of-episode outputs

  • The episode results hold total_xp (lifetime XP per seat) beside the scores. The score itself is 1 per winning-team seat and 0 otherwise3 8.
  • The replay's telemetry records GoldMetric (gold earned from kills, not net), LevelMetric, and kill / death / assist counters per seat1 9.
  • An assist goes to each enemy hero that damaged the victim in the last 10 seconds. It gives no reward9.

finishCoworld adds "total_xp": [...] to the results JSON in seat order when the game supplies it3. totalXp in the simulation returns the lifetime XP of each hero1. This is diagnostic output. The league standing uses the binary win score and platform Elo, per the guide and the standings page of the lab.

Inside the simulation, CombatStats.hitHero records the tick of the last hit by each enemy hero on each victim. On a kill, it adds a loss to the victim, a kill to the attacker if the attacker is a hero, and an assist to every other enemy hero whose last hit was in the last tickRate * 10 ticks9. Tower and footman kills pass attacker -1, so they record a death with no kill credit1. Only the three reward sites increment GoldMetric, so it is equal to the cumulative gold earned, not the current balance1. LevelMetric is a snapshot of the current level1. A replay expander can recover these counters exactly, because they are part of the hashed state.

8 · Consequences for policy design

  • Last hits are the whole economy. Damage that does not kill earns nothing, so the time of the last hit matters more than damage throughput.
  • Hero kills are the high-value target. One is worth six footmen and a tower, and they repeat every 216 ticks.
  • Level scaling is linear and small. Equipment is the fast way to convert early gold into stats.
  • Death is cheap in resources but expensive in tempo. The bounty given away is the real cost.

A policy can see objectHp for every object in vision, and the reward goes to whoever takes the target to zero. Thus a policy can aim for last hits: attack the enemy footman whose HP is at or below the current basic damage of the hero, or use a spell or a poison on one that an ally or a tower is about to kill. The starter attacks the nearest enemy and takes the last hits that happen by chance.

A hero kill at level 1 pays a full level. Even at level 10 it pays a fifth of a level plus 100 gold, which is a pair of Ranger Boots or most of a Sapphire Ring. Respawn is fixed at 216 ticks at every level. Thus repeated kills on the same over-extended enemy are the fastest income in the game.

Buy equipment early. The 150 starting gold buys Leather Gauntlets (+4 damage) and a ration on tick one, and +4 damage is almost one level of growth for most classes (Appendix B). Purchases work anywhere, so there is no reason to walk to the base to shop.

The zero-on-timeout rule limits the value of farming. Gold and XP that do not become a fort kill before tick 28,800 are worth nothing. A policy must use the economy as a means to win fights and sieges faster, and change to objective play when it has the stats to take a lane.
