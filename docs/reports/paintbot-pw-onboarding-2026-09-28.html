Research Report · 2026-09-28 · Onboarding for James

Paintbot PW: an onboarding guide

For James, before the selection of the first Paintbot PW policy. We did the research against Metta-AI/paintbot-pw ab597b35 (tag coworld-v0.3.67). Both leagues used that release when we wrote this report.

Executive summary

Paintbot PW is Paintbot, rebuilt in Polyworld. Polyworld is a deterministic game engine that is written in Nim. Sixteen cogs (the name of the game for its small wheeled robots, one for each seat) play in two teams of eight. Red has the even seats and Blue has the odd seats. The teams fight for ten heart towers on an island with the name Heartwick. Each seat is a small BASIC program, and the game itself runs that program one time in each tick. Thus a "player" is a text file. It is not a container, and it is not a network client. A cog that stays near a heart for three seconds captures it. Owned hearts fill the meter of the team. The first team to 900 meter points wins, or the team with the higher meter wins at ten minutes. Fights are short and dangerous. Each cog has three hit points, four lives, and a paint gun that fires one shot each second. Friendly fire is on, and each cog can see only forward, in a cone.

For strategy, the most important fact is this: the winner and the score are two different things. The heart meter decides the winner. But the platform receives a different number, glory. Glory starts at 600, decreases by one each second, and gets a small number of unusual awards. The loser gets zero glory, and a draw gives zero to both sides. Then the league uses these scores for Elo as a simple win, draw, or loss, and it ignores the margin. As a result, only a win changes the rank. A slow win with no glory left counts as a draw, and a BASIC compile error causes the failure of the full episode. The policy language is a very small BASIC that has only integers. It has a large budget for each tick (50,000 instructions, not the 20,000 that the guide still gives). It also has observations that fog controls, and a small set of actions. The engine changes many times each day. But its replays play again correctly across versions, so analysis with a hash check is easy and fast.

1 · What Paintbot PW is, and what it is not

  • It is a separate coworld, paintbot-pw. It has its own source repository (Metta-AI/paintbot-pw), leagues, forum, and wiki.
  • It uses Polyworld, the same family of Nim engine as Gods of the Arena. It is not the Season 1 capture-the-heart shooter, and it is not the Season 2 battle royale that paintbot_lab studies.
  • The runtime is player_runtime: game-hosted. You upload one BASIC file (or a neural-BASIC ZIP), and the game runs it. There is no player container and no network protocol.
  • It has two leagues: the primary teams ladder paintbot-pw, and Heartland. Heartland is a free-for-all "kin" variant that opened on 2026-09-28.

Three different games on the platform use the Paintbot name. Thus it is easy to move an assumption from one game to a different game by mistake. The older paintbot coworlds are the Season 1 capture-the-heart shooter and the Season 2 battle royale with WASM "plays". The Stencil player of the lab is for those games1. Paintbot PW keeps the look (paint, cogs, hearts). But it makes the game again in Polyworld, with different rules, a different player format, and a different replay format. As a result, nothing from paintbot_lab is correct here until you do a new check of it2.

The source repository is a standalone copy of Polyworld. Its history starts at "initial import of polyworld code" (2026-08-25). The game itself is in examples/paintbot/ (approximately 11,700 lines of Nim), and the code that connects it to the platform is in coworld/paintbot/. The deployed manifest points to the repository address, but it does not give a commit. Thus the release tag coworld-v<version> is the only link from a release to its source3.

How to upload. The player protocol in the manifest accepts "one raw UTF-8 BASIC source file or a neural BASIC ZIP containing manifest.json, policy.bas, and model.bin"4. Before version 0.3.33, the game also accepted WASM. Now a WASM file causes the seat to forfeit5.

2 · A match from start to finish

  • There are 16 seats, and the team of a seat is the seat number mod 2. Even seats are Red ("Ember"), and odd seats are Blue ("Azure").
  • The game has 24 ticks each second. A teams match has a maximum of 14,400 ticks (10:00).
  • There are ten equal hearts. At the start, each team owns one heart, and eight hearts are neutral.
  • A capture takes 72 ticks (3 s) with no opponent, within 140 units. More cogs do not make the capture faster.
  • The meter of a team increases by one tick-point for each owned heart in each tick. At 21,600 tick-points, the team wins. The viewer shows this value as 900 points (tick-points ÷ 24). That is five hearts for three minutes.
  • A team is eliminated when it has no cog alive and no lives left. Then the meter of the other team becomes full immediately.

Seats and teams

There are 16 seats6, and the team of a seat is simply slot mod 26. The manifest lets a configuration give a team label to each seat, but the engine never reads those labels7. In the primary league, each episode puts two policies against each other. One policy fills all eight even seats, and the other fills all eight odd seats. Thus a team is eight copies of one file8. The seats make decisions in the order of their numbers. But on odd ticks, each even and odd pair changes places, so neither team always moves first9.

Hearts and capture

A cog "touches" a heart when three conditions are true. The cog is alive. The cog is within 140 units (1.4 m, because one unit is one centimeter). And the cog has a clear path to the heart, with no step in height of more than 25 units in each 20-unit sample9,6. The progress of a capture follows a small state machine.

Source for Figure 19. Territory is the colored region on the screen near each heart. In the teams game, territory has no effect on the play. Its increase in speed and accuracy occurs only in Heartland6. Each script can read the positions of the hearts, their owners, and the progress of each capture (section 7).

Lives and spawning

Each cog has 3 base hit points and 4 lives: the first life and three more lives6,9. A dead cog comes back after 72 ticks, and it has 36 ticks of spawn protection. A death removes the grenade, the spray can, the armor, and the disguise of the cog6,9. If a team owns hearts, its cogs come back within 350 units of one of those hearts. The engine selects the heart with a softmax that prefers hearts far from the teammates that are alive. If a team owns no hearts, its cogs come back in the end zone of the team6.

How a match ends

Each end of a match goes through one line. That line sets the winner from the meters, and then it calls settleGlory9. An elimination decides the match, because the engine first makes the meter of the survivor full. If both teams are eliminated in the same tick, the engine compares the meters as they are9.

3 · Winning versus scoring: glory

  • The meter selects the winner. The score is glory, and only the team that wins keeps it.
  • Glory starts at 600 (the length of the match in seconds). It decreases by 1 each second, to a minimum of 0.
  • Three awards add to it: +10 for each 30 s with no team pickup, +20 for each glory heart (a temporary bonus pickup, not a control heart), and +5 for each life behind in each 5 s in the league configuration.
  • The Elo of the league changes each episode into a win, a draw, or a loss. It compares the average scores of the two sides and ignores the margin.
  • The result: a win at 10:00 with no awards gets 0. That is equal to the 0 of the loser, so Elo records a draw.

A glory heart is a temporary bonus pickup. It is not one of the ten control hearts that decide the match. The two items have the same word in their names, but they have no other relation.

Two details are important for a policy that tries to get glory. First, the clock for quiet supplies starts again only when a teammate actually takes a pickup. A cog takes a pickup only when the pickup is useful (for example, a medkit only when the cog is hurt). Thus a cog at full health can walk over a medkit, and the clock does not start again9. Second, glory hearts come in mirrored pairs from 0:20, every 10–20 s. Each one stays for 30 s, and fog hides glory hearts as it hides other pickups6. Each teams variant in the manifest sets glory.behind_lives to 5. The default of the engine is 1, and local runs through local.py cannot change it10.

From episode score to league rank

The Elo code of the ladder compares the average scores of the two sides and records 1, 0.5, or 011. It uses K = 32 and a start rating of 1,5008. If an episode fails and the platform connects the failure to one policy, that side gets a forfeit loss11. In practice:

  • Win, and win before glory gets to zero. A win at time t seconds gives approximately 600 − t, plus awards. In one live league round, the winners got 428–577 in all 12 episodes. Thus the wins of today are fast8.
  • The size of glory is not the rank. Awards are important only near zero, where a win with 0 glory becomes a draw.
  • A team behind in lives gets glory, but only if it also wins. A team that is 8 lives behind for one minute gets 12 × 40 = 480 glory. That glory has no value to a loser.

4 · Combat and the battlefield

  • One unit is one centimeter. A body has a radius of 55 units and moves 28 units in each tick.
  • The gun: a 5-tick windup, then a hitscan ray to 5,250 units. It fires one shot each second, and friendly fire is on.
  • A spray can replaces the gun, and it never becomes empty. A grenade goes over walls and causes damage to all bodies in the blast, and that includes the cog that threw it.
  • Armor, or a position in a trench, makes the gun cooldown three times longer.
  • Vision is a 120° cone to the front. It has no limit in distance, but terrain stops it. Sound gives only a direction and a distance band.

Movement and terrain

A cog moves 28 units in each tick. Sneak makes this speed half, water makes it one quarter, and a cog that leaves a trench moves five times slower on each axis6,9. Bodies block all other cogs, and a blocked cog moves to the side6,9. The engine finds the path for you on a one-meter grid. On that grid, a lake cell costs four times more than a dry cell. Since rules 45, the engine also sends a cog into water when the goal itself is in water, and that change lets cogs get to the lake heart6. The area of Heartwick that cogs can use is 160 × 96 m. It is symmetric under a half turn about (3200, 2000), and it has an inland lake6.

As a result, most misses occur because the target moves during the windup (we calculated this from the limit of the random error)9. For this reason, the baseline policy aims ahead of its target and moves in short random legs (section 9).

Thus a policy gets items when it walks to them, and it avoids items when it walks around them. Trenches are pits that cogs can walk into. A cog leaves a trench slowly. Vision and hearing

Each cog sees a 120° cone around its current aim point. The cone has no limit in distance, but cover and terrain block it from an eye height of 120 units6. The aim point stays from one tick to the next. lookAt and shootAt set it. If a tick has no aim call, walkTo sets it9. Thus a cog sees only the direction that it faces, and a cog that walks turns to face the direction of its walk. The rules also include vision that all cogs of a team share, but only as an option, and no deployed variant uses it.

Sound comes from footsteps (1,000 units, but not during sneak), gun shots (3,500), explosions (5,000), and spray (1,800). A sound gives only its type, one of eight compass sectors, a distance band, and its age. It never tells who made the sound9. Speech is different. A shout goes to all cogs within 12.8 m that are alive on both teams, and each listener gets the exact position of the speaker12.

5 · How a policy runs

  • Each seat has one file and one virtual machine (VM), and the VMs do not share memory. Eight copies of your file are one team.
  • In each tick, each seat that is alive runs its script from the first line. Dead cogs do not run.
  • Global variables and arrays stay from one tick to the next. The string pool and the command of the tick do not stay.
  • All 16 scripts read the same frozen world. Then the world moves one step with all their commands.
  • The engine keeps some intent from one tick to the next. The walk goal and the aim point stay, but the trigger does not stay.

Sources12,7. The restart() function of the VM clears the registers and the budgets, but it keeps the globals and the arrays13. Thus the setup that occurs one time is a check of a flag at the top of the file, as in base.bas:

if started = 0 then
  started = 1
  rngState = selfId * 4099 + 977
  lastX = selfX
  lastY = selfY
end if
myVX = selfX - lastX
myVY = selfY - lastY

What persists between ticks

The engine clears the commands in each tick12. But the cog itself keeps some state9:

Call Persists?
walkTo(x, y)Yes. The cog keeps walking to the goal until a new walkTo or a respawn.
lookAt(x, y) / shootAt(x, y) aimYes. The aim point, which is also the centre of the vision cone.
shootAt triggerNo. Call it on the tick you want to fire.
chargeGrenade(1)No. The charge is kept, but a tick without the call throws the grenade.
sneak(1)No. Call it every tick.

Remember the grenade row, because it is a trap. A script can charge a grenade, and then, in the next tick, go into a branch that does not call chargeGrenade(1). In that condition, the cog throws the grenade in the direction of its current aim.

6 · The BASIC dialect

  • The language is the BASIC of Polyworld. It has only signed 32-bit integers, IF/WHILE/SUB, fixed arrays, and PRINT.
  • It has no FOR and no user-defined functions that return values (only SUB). The calls of the host, for example controlX(i), do return values. It has no built-in math (ABS, SQR, RND), no floating point, and strings only as handles.
  • An integer overflow wraps with no error. A division by zero or an array index out of range is a runtime error, and it stops the seat.
  • The budget for each decision is 50,000 instructions and 125,000 work units. The guide and the headers of the starter files still give 20,000.
  • The fixed limits are 128 KiB of source, 512 globals, 4,096 array elements in total, and a call depth of 16.

The dialect is small, so this report can list all of it. The reserved words are and call dim else end exit false gosub goto if let mod not or print rem return stop sub then true wend while xor. The words goto and gosub are reserved, but the compiler makes no code for them13.

Feature Behavior
ValuesSigned int32. + - * wrap on overflow; / and MOD truncate toward zero13.
Precedence, low to highOR XOR, AND, comparisons, + -, * / MOD, unary - + NOT. NOT binds tighter than =, so write NOT (a = b)13.
VariablesGlobal, created on first use, start at 0. Sub parameters are local; anything else assigned in a SUB is global13.
ArraysDIM name(N) at top level with a literal bound, inclusive: DIM a(15) has 16 cells13.
Control flowIF cond THEN … [ELSE] … END IF; WHILE cond … WEND13.
SubroutinesSUB name(a, b) … END SUB, no return values: return results through globals13.
PrintingPRINT "a"; x to the seat's private log; 1,024 bytes and 128 print events per decision, or the seat is disabled13,12.

The language has no square root. Thus base.bas has its own integer isqrt with Newton's method, and that subroutine gives its result in the global root. All helpers give their results in the same way: a SUB writes a global.

A complete minimal policy

The smallest useful policy is short. It sends each cog to the nearest heart that its team does not own, and it shoots the nearest enemy that it can see:

' Minimal Paintbot PW policy: go to the nearest heart we do not own,
' and shoot the nearest enemy we can see.
target = -1
best = 2147483647
i = 0
while i < heartCount()
  if controlOwner(i) <> selfTeam then
    dx = (controlX(i) - selfX) / 10
    dy = (controlY(i) - selfY) / 10
    if dx * dx + dy * dy < best then
      best = dx * dx + dy * dy
      target = i
    end if
  end if
  i = i + 1
wend
if target >= 0 then
  walkTo(controlX(target), controlY(target))
end if

foe = -1
best = 2147483647
i = 0
while i < 16
  if i mod 2 <> selfTeam and visible(i) then
    dx = (playerX(i) - selfX) / 10
    dy = (playerY(i) - selfY) / 10
    if dx * dx + dy * dy < best then
      best = dx * dx + dy * dy
      foe = i
    end if
  end if
  i = i + 1
wend
if foe >= 0 then
  shootAt(playerX(foe), playerY(foe))
end if

This short example shows three habits of the dialect. First, the code divides each distance by 10 before it squares it. This keeps the arithmetic safe on the big generated maps, where 50,000 units squared overflows int32 (on Heartwick, an overflow cannot occur). Second, the code gets the team from the seat number (i mod 2). A uniform can deceive this check, as it can deceive playerTeam, because a disguised enemy uses the seat number of a teammate. Third, the script starts again from the top in each tick, so it does not need a primary loop.

This policy compiles and runs. We ran two local matches against base.bas for this report, one on each side (seeds 2026 and 2027, engine build 0.3.65, paintbot-headless --bot). Both games stopped by tick 1,379, in less than one minute. The meter cannot become full so quickly, so an elimination ended each game. At the end, base.bas held more hearts (4 to 1 and 5 to 1). This large difference is the reason to start from base.bas and not from nothing.

7 · What a policy can see and do

  • Always public: the positions of the hearts, their owners, the state of each capture, the glory of both teams, the map geometry (bounds, height, water, trenches), and the number of pickup stations.
  • Fog controls: other cogs, ready pickups, and glory hearts. A script can see them only in its cone.
  • Actions: walkTo, lookAt, shootAt, chargeGrenade, sneak, and shout.
  • A disguised enemy uses the seat number that it copies. heardX/Y give the exact position of a speaker.
  • Each query gives −1 (or 0 for HP) when the item is hidden or not correct. Thus a hidden cog and a missing cog look the same.

The host API is a set of read-only values and functions with names, and all of them give int32 values12. Coordinates are in world units, and "Y" is the second horizontal axis, which the engine calls z. Appendix B has the full list. The general shape is this:

Some details change how you write scripts:

  • A script cannot read the gun cooldown of its cog. Thus base.bas keeps its own countdown (gunWait) and sets it to zero after a respawn.
  • A pickup that is not ready looks the same as a pickup out of view. Both give −1. Thus a policy records the time of each pickup that it sees.
  • nearAgents costs less than a loop of visible(i) in large games. The file nearby.bas15 in the repository is base.bas written again in that way14.
  • Six fields are dead. carrying, heartX/Y, ownHeartX/Y, ownHeartStolen, and playerCarrying come from the capture-the-flag rules before rules 13. Under rules 45, their values never change9. base.bas still reads playerCarrying. This causes no damage, but it can confuse a reader.

8 · How policies fail

  • If the host does not accept a file (WASM, more than 128 KiB, not UTF-8, bad ZIP), only that seat forfeits. The other 15 seats continue.
  • A BASIC compile error causes the failure of the full episode, and no seat gets results.
  • A runtime error (the budget is exceeded, a division by zero, a bad index, the print limit) stops that seat for the remaining part of the episode. The match continues.
  • A stopped cog continues to walk to its last goal, but it never shoots again. It still counts in the lives of its team.
Failure Consequence Evidence
Host rejects the fileSeat forfeits; idle stub; episode scores normally5
Compile error (syntax, unknown name, FFA-only call in the teams game)Engine writes failure.json naming the slot and stops without results12,16
Neural model rejected at loadSeat never runs, not even its BASIC12
Runtime errorSeat disabled; "BASIC error: …" in its log; status "BASIC VM disabled"12

The compile error is the most important row. In a local test, a seat with a syntax error wrote {"message":"BASIC compilation failed for player slot 1","failed_policy_index":1} and gave no results. Then local.py stopped only at its deadline of ten minutes10. The failure record gives a policy index. Thus it is possible that the league connects the failure to the side that uploaded the file, and the Elo code gives that side a forfeit loss. We did not see this connection in a live league. The guide says that "the episode still starts" when a seat fails, but this is true only when the host does not accept the file. A call to a Heartland-only function such as kin() in the teams game is also a compile error. Thus, if one file calls those names, it cannot operate in both modes.

We know the behavior of a stopped cog only from the movement code, not from a test. The cog gives no new commands. Thus it continues to walk to its last goal with its last aim, and it never fires. After its next death, it stays at its spawn point. It still counts in the lives of its team, so opponents can kill it again and again9.

9 · The starter policies

  • base.bas (677 lines) is the baseline of the league and the reference design of the maintainer. Start with this file.
  • It uses habits that the maintainer measured one at a time: aim ahead of the target, never walk in a straight line when an opponent can see you, move in squads of four, refuse fights that you will lose, and go around water.
  • jev.bas is base.bas with an LLM advisor layer. Without an advisor, it plays the same as base.bas.
  • ffa.bas and ffa_blind.bas are only for Heartland. They do not compile in the teams game.
  • base.bas ignores glory fully. It takes supplies and never goes to glory hearts.

base.bas

All cogs run the same file. In each tick, each cog does ten steps17,18:

  1. Scan. The cog looks at all 16 seats for the best enemy that it can see within 52.5 m, with a preference for hurt enemies. It also counts the foes within 26 m and the friends within 12 m17.
  2. Squads. The seats make two squads of four from (selfId / 2) mod 8. Each squad selects the nearest heart that it does not own, as measured from a point above or below home. Blue uses the mirrored point. Two members stand in the ring, and two members give cover from outside. The members do not talk: each member calculates the same selection from the public ownership of the hearts17.
  3. Supplies. The cog remembers each pickup that it saw for 10 s. When no enemy is near, it goes to get a grenade if it has none, a medkit if it is hurt, or armor if it has full HP17.
  4. Refuse bad fights. If more foes than friends are near, the cog goes to the heart that is far from the foes17.
  5. Look. If the cog has no target, it looks in four directions, one after the other. It can also turn to the last speaker, or to the sector of the last gun shot17.
  6. Footwork. In a fight, the cog walks random legs of 3–9 ticks across the line of fire. It fires only at the start of a leg that is long enough for the windup17.
  7. Dry route. If the line to a target goes across water, the cog goes around through the best of six side points17.
  8. Aim. The cog aims ahead of the target by six ticks of its last movement. It removes its own movement from the aim. If a teammate is within 95 units of the line, it does not fire17.
  9. Grenade. The cog charges to agree with the distance (4–12.5 m), but not when a teammate is near the target. It does not do a check of its own distance to the blast17.
  10. Sneak. The cog sneaks near the objective when it hears something but sees nothing17.

The measurements of the maintainer show why each habit is there. The new base.bas won 100–0 against the previous baseline. When the maintainer removed one habit, the policy lost: aim 3–37, footwork 12–28, refusal of fights 12–28, squads 16–2414 (not tested again by us). Under rules 37, the dry-route habit gave 89 wins of 120, against 12 of 60 without it. But rules 38 made the routes of the engine avoid slow water, and the advantage of the habit decreased to 59–4114.

jev.bas and the Heartland starters

jev.bas (2,644 lines) adds an advisor layer to base.bas. One cog in each squad asks the oracle (section 10) to select the heart of the squad from three candidates. Then it tells the squad the selection with a shout ("Alpha, push Forge."), and its squadmates use that selection19. All cogs within 12.8 m can hear a shout. Thus an enemy that is near can learn the plan. The header of jev.bas still gives a budget of 20,000 instructions. ffa.bas is the baseline for Heartland, and it calls functions that operate only in FFA, such as kin(). ffa_blind.bas is a generated control that thinks that all other cogs are strangers (Appendix A).

10 · The other two lanes: advisor oracle and neural BASIC

  • Advisor oracle: a script can ask an LLM, through the host, a yes/no question, a score question, or a selection question. The question does not stop the script, and the answers come some ticks later.
  • The oracle uses the LLM budget of the seat for the episode. We do not know if the paintbot-pw leagues give a budget. If they give none, the host refuses each question, and jev.bas plays the same as base.bas.
  • Neural BASIC: a ZIP of manifest.json, policy.bas, and model.bin (up to 16 MiB). In each tick, the script calls native FP32 inference, and the engine uses the output as the command of the cog.
  • The current #2 on the ladder has the name daveey-pw-neural. Thus it is possible that this lane is competitive (we think this only from the name).

Advisor oracle

A seat makes a request during one decision: state fields, notes, questions, and criteria. It sends the request with oracleAsk() and keeps the request id in a global. Then, in later ticks, it calls oraclePoll(id) to get the answers20. Each seat can have only one request at a time. There must be a minimum of 24 ticks between two questions, and the host clears the draft of a request at the start of each tick20. On hosted leagues, the host sends the request to the LLM sidecar of the platform with a deadline of two seconds. The cost comes from the LLM budget of the seat for the episode. A seat with no budget gets no answers21. The host writes each question and answer to the private log of the seat. The maintainer measured that the Jev advisor won 20 of 24 full matches against base.bas through the hosted route, with approximately 100 questions in each game14.

Neural BASIC

A neural upload is a ZIP. Its policy.bas usually makes three calls in each tick:

paintbot_observe(neuralObservation())
run_neural_net(neuralModel(), neuralObservation(), neuralLogits(), neuralState())
paintbot_act(neuralLogits())

These calls cost 512, 16, and 128 work units. A script must call each one a maximum of one time in each tick, in this sequence. An incorrect use stops the seat22. paintbot_act replaces the full command for the tick. Thus the engine ignores action calls before it, and action calls after it change it. The model takes 448 inputs (506 in version 2 of the observation contract, which adds terrain). It gives 82 logits in heads of 51, 25, 2, 2, and 2. It can use a maximum of 4,000,000 counted operations, and the engine does this check one time, at load. Its recurrent state starts again at the start of the match and at each death22. The full contract is in neural_basic.md23 and neural_actor.md24 in the repository. The repository also has a training bridge25.

11 · Engine nuances and traps

  • The game changes many times each day, and league episodes use each new release immediately.
  • A replay is a full record of the actions, with a state hash for each tick. The newest build plays older versions of the rules again exactly.
  • The guide does not agree with the code in some places. In each of those places, the code is correct.
  • outcome: "time_limit" means a draw. It does not mean "the clock stopped the match".
  • On the big generated maps, squared distances overflow int32.
  • All league episodes that we examined used seed 2026.

A moving target

The repository released 40 versions in the ten days from 0.3.25 to 0.3.65. Rules 34 through 45 each changed the play: losses by elimination, the mirrored fair map, glory, glory hearts, stronger grenades, generated maps, shared team vision, glory that you can configure, and cogs that walk in water to the lake heart26. Releases are automatic. On the day of this report, 0.3.65 became 0.3.67, and 0.3.68 (ef82196) went live in the evening. 0.3.68 changes no rules. It moves the code that reads the match configuration into a new file, match_config.nim, and it adds a LiveRules = 45 constant. As a result, some line numbers in game.nim and sim.nim move, but the citations in this report stay exact at ab597b35. On the same evening, a third league (Heartland Big) appeared, and main got a commit that makes Heartland a separate coworld. The deployed_ref.py3 script of the lab finds the source commit for the release of each league. Run it before you trust a rule, and that includes the rules in this report.

Replays re-simulate across versions

A hosted replay is a POLYWORLDREPLAY tape. It has the seed, the command of each seat in each tick, a state hash for each tick, the names of the players, the full text of each shout, and the match configuration27,7. The header of the tape records the number of the rules, and the simulator keeps the code for all older rules. Thus one current build plays older games again, and the hashes agree exactly. The 0.3.65 build of the lab played three rules-44 league games again with zero hash errors. When we forced rules 45 on the same tapes, the hashes did not agree within 26–269 ticks10. Gods of the Arena is different: its replays play again correctly only at the exact commit of the recording. The replay_stats tool of the repository does not do a check of the hashes. Thus it can give numbers that look correct for a replay that did not play again correctly.

Smaller traps

  • time_limit means a draw. matchOutcome gives "time_limit" each time that there is no winner. A match that the meter decides at 10:00 gives "0" or "1"7.
  • int32 on big maps. base.bas says that "23170² exceeds any squared map distance". That is not true on big-twin-mesas and big-deep-forest (approximately ten times the area of Heartwick), where squared distances wrap (we calculated this from the size of the maps). The leagues play on Heartwick at this time.
  • Fixed seed. All league episodes that we examined used seed 2026 (3 teams episodes and 2 Heartland episodes). Thus it is possible that the map, the positions of the items, and the times of the glory hearts are almost the same in all league games. We do not know yet if the engine seeds are different for each job.
  • Damage from your own grenade. The blast of a grenade includes the cog that threw it, and base.bas does not do a check of its own distance.
  • Armor makes your gun slower. While armor stays on a cog, the gun cooldown is three times longer. Thus armor gives more life but less damage.
