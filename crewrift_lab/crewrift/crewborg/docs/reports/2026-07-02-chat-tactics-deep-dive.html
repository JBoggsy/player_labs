Crewrift Prime · chat tactics deep-dive

What are the effective chatters actually doing?

Read the real chat text (not just the classified accusation rows) behind the top-3 and bottom-3 policies by same-meeting ejection conversion. The answer isn't more evidence or smarter reasoning — it's silence, rigidity, and an explicit call to action.

Same 200-episode fresh pull as the parent study. 481 meetings where one of the six policies below spoke; every message read, not sampled. Six policies: the top-3 by ejection conversion (jordan-crewborg-aaln, crewborg-mv, relhalpha-hunter) and three comparison points (crewborg, rowdaboat-notsus, softmaxwell-crewborg — the weakest of the six).

01The headline pattern

Three variables, read across all six policies' actual chat, line up almost perfectly with same-meeting ejection conversion: how often they stay silent, whether they close with an explicit vote X, and whether they speak first.

"Vote when speaking" and "speaks first" are computed over substantive (non-silent) messages only.

The exact opposite of intuition. The three most effective chatters say almost nothing — 82–87% of every meeting they simply pass ("no read, skipping"). Across 1,166 sampled messages from all three, there are exactly two distinct things any of them ever say: that pass, or one fixed template — "saw COLOR, COLOR sus, vote COLOR". Zero hedging, zero questions, zero defending, zero elaboration. crewborg, by contrast, never passes (0% silent) and has 83 distinct message shapes — it is chattier and more analytically varied than all three top performers combined, and it converts the worst.

02The vocabulary, side by side

The top style — one template, said or not said

"no read, skipping" — 84.1% of all messages (n=1,166 across the 3 policies)
"saw green, green sus, vote green" — 15.9% (color varies, structure never does)

crewborg — rich, varied, evidence-heavy, and never closes

"pink sus: lurking on a vent, they were tailing me"
"Red, you were with Pink for 46 ticks around tick 861-906 in Bridge. Pink hasn't moved since tick 907. Where are you now?"
"Green and Purple are dead. Yellow was in Hydroponics 120 ticks — longest stay. Red, Blue nearby same time. Where was Yellow really?"

Checked for alternate closing language too (eject/kick/remove/get out) — 0 of 244 messages. crewborg names a suspect and cites real, specific evidence for it, then simply stops. It never tells the room what to do with what it just said.

rowdaboat-notsus — investigative, interrogates the room, moderate follow-through

"Purple called this out of nowhere. What did you see, purple?"
"Blue was near a kill and now deflecting onto me. Vote blue."

340 distinct shapes across 415 messages — genuinely responsive to what's happening, asks direct questions of the meeting-caller, and closes with an explicit vote about 1 in 5 times (better than crewborg, well behind the top-3).

softmaxwell-crewborg — cautious, forensic, and the field's worst accuracy

"purple vented tick 766. need second cue before voting — anyone else see purple move weird?"
"green & purple dead. blue/cyan were together at bridge doing tasks. red isolated. skip to gather info."

Also mostly silent (89.7%) like the top-3, but its rare messages are the opposite of rigid — precise tick numbers and vent IDs, and explicit hedges ("need second cue") or deliberate deferrals ("skip to gather info"). This is the most epistemically careful of the six — and has the worst accuracy (9.5%) and near-worst conversion in the whole field. Caution about communicating suspicion doesn't fix a weak underlying read on who to suspect.

04Is this persuasion, or just being right?

Split same-meeting ejection by whether the accusation was actually correct — this is the test for whether "effective" chat is really just "accurate" chat wearing a costume.

PolicyEjected% (correct accusation)Ejected% (wrong accusation)Gap

jordan is the outlier that answers the question. Its wrong accusations get ejected almost as often as its right ones (63% vs 66% — essentially no discrimination). The room isn't evaluating truth when jordan speaks; it's following a confident, terse, first-mover, unhedged call — this is anchoring, not persuasion-by-evidence. crewborg-mv and relhalpha show more normal correctness-sensitivity but still convert wrong calls at rates crewborg and rowdaboat never reach even when right. Meanwhile crewborg and rowdaboat show the healthiest correctness-discrimination in the field (biggest relative right/wrong gaps) — the room is comparatively harder to convince when they're wrong — but their overall persuasive power is much weaker across the board. Style and substance are separate axes here, and in this field, style is currently winning.

05What this suggests for crewborg

Two structural gaps, independent of whether crewborg's underlying suspicion model is any good (it may be — its correctness-discrimination is actually reasonable):

  1. It never closes. 0.8% of its messages include an explicit vote (checked eject/kick/remove/get out too — still 0). It names a suspect with real, specific evidence and then stops. Appending an explicit call to action costs essentially nothing and is the single variable most correlated with conversion in this data.
  2. It never speaks first. 0% first-mover rate across 244 messages, vs. 30–36% for the top-3. When suspicion is already high before a meeting even opens, waiting to hear everyone else first cedes the anchor to whoever speaks first — which, per this data, is often enough to win the room regardless of who's actually right.

Not a verdict, a hypothesis. Both are meeting/chat-generation changes, not suspicion-model changes — genuinely separable from the open evidence-warming suspicion lever. Neither has been designed or A/B'd yet; this is read-and-pattern evidence from one 200-episode window, and the field's top-3 sharing a byte-identical template means this is really one observed winning strategy, not three independent confirmations. Worth a small, cheap, pre-registered test before trusting it further.
