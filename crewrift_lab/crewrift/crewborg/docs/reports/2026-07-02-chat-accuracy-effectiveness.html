Crewrift Prime · field-wide study

Is chat actually accurate — and does it actually help?

Every player's crew accusations checked against the true imposter, every accusation traced through to the vote and the ejection, then to the seat-normalized win rate. Two datasets, one live-Bedrock-validated detector, one bug found and fixed along the way.

Fresh pull: 200 episodes, 2026‑07‑02
Field: 8 current Prime champions
Historical cross‑check: 2,976 episodes (June 12–13)
Detector validation: 200/200 chat lines vs Bedrock

01What was measured

Two questions, asked field‑wide rather than about any one policy. Accuracy (crew only): when a crew member accuses someone, how often is the accused actually the imposter? Effectiveness (crew and imposter): does the accusation move the room — does the accused get voted, does the accused get ejected — and does accusing correlate with winning?

This is observational, not causal — nobody was randomly assigned to accuse or stay silent, so every relationship below is an association, not a proven effect.

Data. A fresh 200‑episode pull (2 experience requests of 100, pinned to the current top‑8 Prime champions by leaderboard rank, natural roles, rotating seats) plus a 2,976‑episode historical cross‑check from the existing suspicion_lab corpus (June 12–13 — the most recent scraped batch, June 25, turned out to be missing results.json entirely and couldn't be used). Both were parsed with the same accusation‑extraction code, reusing this lab's existing chat‑stance regex read‑only rather than re‑implementing chat parsing.

A bug, not a finding. The first pass through this table showed every single row at exactly 0% ejected — a suspiciously uniform split that turned out to be a real, order‑sensitive bug in the replay parser's per‑meeting ejection field (the game's own "meeting closed" event fires the same tick as the ejection and gets processed first, so the field silently never got set — 0 of 73 meetings in a live sample). Fixed by deriving ejection from the raw ejection log instead of the broken per‑meeting field; all numbers above are post‑fix. Recorded as a durable lesson since anything else touching that field inherits the same silent zero.

crewborg's own accusations land the accused in a vote 76–88% of the time (both roles) — the room listens — but only converts to an actual ejection 18–22% of the time, well below jordan/crewborg‑mv/relhalpha's 53–65%. Chat is heard; it isn't closing.

Directionally consistent, not statistically significant. Both datasets point the same way — more accurate accusing associates with a somewhat higher crew win rate — but at only 8–14 policies compared, neither correlation clears conventional significance. Accusation volume, if anything, trends the other way: chatting more isn't the lever, chatting right might be. This lines up with an existing observation in this lab: the single best historical crew win rate (Andre, ~ejections‑driven, near‑zero chat) came from task completion, not talking. Don't oversell this section — it needs a much larger policy roster to say anything with confidence.

05How much to trust the detector

The accusation‑target detector is a cheap regex (first color named in an accuse/defend line) — not ground truth. Validated it against the event‑warehouse's existing Bedrock‑backed chat labeler on all 200 fresh episodes' chat.

A manual 10‑row spot‑check matched this: 9/10 clearly correct, one miss on a message naming three colors before the actual subject ("Blue dead... Green was with blue... Pink sus: no alibi, quick vote") — the regex grabbed Blue (first color mentioned) instead of Pink (the one actually being accused). Read every accuracy number above with roughly a 12–point margin for detector noise, not as an exact figure.

What this doesn't show

No causal claim — nothing here randomizes who accuses whom. The win‑rate association is under‑powered at 8–14 policies. The historical cross‑check predates today's policy versions. And two real methodology gaps were found and either fixed (the ejection bug) or disclosed (the join‑key mismatch between this pipeline's episode‑stem convention and the event‑warehouse's internal id, caught before it could silently zero out the detector validation on a paid Bedrock run) — full detail in crewrift_lab/chat_effectiveness/README.md and this session's TENTATIVE_LESSONS.md entries.
